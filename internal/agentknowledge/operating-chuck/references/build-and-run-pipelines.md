# Build and run pipelines

Pipelines are saved, model-neutral templates whose stages run as bounded durable assignments to one standing run orchestrator, with persisted stage outputs and operator recovery.

- A template's ordered stages are the usual plan for reaching the goal, not a fixed script; there is no condition language or graph editor. Validate a template before starting it. A stage may be marked `required_for_completion` (an explicit check the run cannot complete without) or keep a human approval gate, which also makes it required. Starting a run authorizes its ordinary steps and their necessary repair and re-review without further clicks.
- The standing owner judges the next step and reports it with its stage result through `report_task_result.pipeline_decision`: `next` with a listed `stage_id` (the usual next step, a repeat, or a revisit such as Fix then Review again), `complete` with `required_checks` evidence for every required stage (required at the plan's end), or `needs_input` with one concrete question. A success without a decision takes the usual next step. `get_assigned_task` returns `pipeline` (steps, current visit, required-check evidence, recovery allowance) and `pipeline_contract`; read them rather than assuming the order. A refused decision leaves the assignment open for correction.
- A failed or `blocked` result without a decision returns to the owner as a recovery visit carrying that evidence; it does not ask the person. Repeating or revisiting a step and each recovery visit spend one of three automatic rounds; forward progress and reporting findings do not. When the allowance is spent the run pauses recoverably and notifies the person, who can repair and Resume (no text needed) or send instructions; either grants a fresh allowance. Explicit Stop, approval gates and permission requests always win.
- Starting a run selects the project, goal, named inputs, and one backend/model for the standing orchestrator. A stage configured for dedicated coordination also selects its sub-orchestrator's role and runtime; other stages supply instructions only, and never a different role or runtime for the standing orchestrator.
- Every stage arrives as an assignment to that same standing orchestrator, carrying the run goal, the stage objective, its context and instructions, the expected output, and the durable outputs earlier stages produced. Conversation continuity is useful context, never the authority for workflow position: read the assignment, not the transcript.
- Only an outcome Chuck accepts from the standing orchestrator holding the active stage can complete that stage. Idle, process exit, and the aggregate state of child work never substitute for its report, and a person cannot mark a stage passed without one. A rejected report leaves the assignment actionable; a duplicate or stale report cannot complete a later assignment.
- Inside the active stage, the standing owner and any delegated coordinator decide decomposition, delegation, reviewer selection, repair routing, and whether re-review is needed. A failed, blocked, cancelled, or replaced child task does not by itself block or advance the pipeline. Repair inside a stage when that is enough; when another listed step is the right repair (for example Fix, then Review again), report the stage and choose it with `pipeline_decision`.
- A template may carry optional `orchestrator_instructions`: run-wide conventions such as naming, grouping or delegation, written once instead of in every stage objective. A run freezes them; Chuck adds them to the standing instructions of that run's freshly launched standing owner and dedicated coordinators, not to stage assignments. They are shared defaults: an explicit exception in a stage assignment overrides them, and they grant no authority, access or permission. Ordinary delegated workers and Think Tank participants do not receive them automatically; pass along what a worker needs when delegating.
- When a stage is configured with dedicated coordination, Chuck creates one managed child task beneath the stage assignment and launches its sub-orchestrator there. That sub-orchestrator is the normal coordination contact and the authoritative source for its delegated work, and it reports upward; its result cannot advance or close the stage. The standing owner evaluates that report and keeps the final stage decision. It may inspect descendants or intervene directly — that transfers no ownership — and should send material intervention as best-effort deferred mail, which reaches the coordinator on its next natural turn.
- A stage configured as a Think Tank runs as a room instead of a standing-owner assignment: fresh participants in the run's project deliberate, then a fresh judge synthesizes after discussion ends. The run start supplies every participant's and the judge's runtime. The room's first entry is the stage context (run goal, objective, inputs and the required output); read it before contributing. Only the judge's published synthesis completes the stage, and its exact text becomes the stage's one named output — completion means a synthesis exists, not that participants agreed. The standing orchestrator gets no turn inside the room; after the room's cleanup (and its approval, if configured) it receives one decision-only assignment for that step that names the room result: read the synthesis, do not redo the room's work, and report success with `pipeline_decision` — the next step, `complete` citing the room task as that step's evidence, or `needs_input`. That assignment produces no outputs and cannot itself be required-check evidence. A route is refused for correction when the chosen step's required inputs have not been produced or a chosen room's context would not fit; unchosen steps are not checked. Recover a held room from the room itself; generic stage Continue, Retry and Replace do not apply, except Retry output acceptance when Chuck could not accept a published synthesis. Stop closes the room and cancels only its in-flight turn.
- At accepted completion the stage closes to new work and its unfinished descendants are cancelled, and cleanup must finish before the next stage starts. Do not plan for child work to outlive the stage that created it. Transient cleanup failures retry automatically, so a run reporting that cleanup is retrying does not need a person.
- Recovery is automatic within the run's allowance. An owner cut off by an unexpected process exit or a Chuck restart is resumed on its same identity; an owner whose start or session can no longer be used is replaced by a fresh owner on the run's frozen runtime, with the old identity and child work retained and the person's pending messages (text and attachments) moved to the replacement. Each spends one round; when the allowance is spent the run pauses as `recovery_exhausted` and notifies the person. An owner whose turns keep ending without a report or wait is continued, then paused as `owner_not_reporting`; running background work defers that check until it ends. A cleanup or controller step that keeps failing pauses as `controller_recovery_failed` with its intent kept. Nothing reports an outcome in the owner's place.
- Only a `needs_input` question, an approval gate or one of those recoverable pauses needs the human. Continue answers the question, approves the gate, or resumes the paused run with optional instructions; an answer or instructions sent in the paused owner's conversation do the same. A message to an owner whose assignment is waiting or starting is queued and delivered with its next turn, and stays visible until then. Retry and Replace remain optional tools for an interrupted owner. None of them rewrites an accepted result, and none continues a blocked stage inside its existing chat turn.
- Stop closes the run to further work and cancels every unfinished stage task and descendant, including queued, waiting, retrying, and nested delegated work. Nothing delegated keeps running afterwards. Completed results stay immutable and the run record is not erased.
- Stage outputs live in Chuck's durable run and task records and stay inspectable after completion. Persisted outputs, not transcripts, are what later stages and supervision read.
- A run may reach several repositories, but only ones the run's existing Chuck project configuration already reaches. The pipeline grants no cross-project access and no additional filesystem roots.
- Owning an active run does not make an agent unaddressable, but stage work reaches the standing orchestrator through its current durable assignment; an unrelated task assignment cannot take that agent over while the run owns it. Normal messaging, resume, archive, and task-target rules apply again once run ownership ends.
- Chucky may propose a template or exact run configuration only for human review. A proposal neither saves nor starts anything; the operator confirms those actions in Chuck. Other roles do not gain proposal authority from this skill.

Use the pipeline tool definitions and CLI help for exact arguments and current result fields.
